Acknowledging Our Sin

By Jared Haftel • August 14, 2026
"If we claim to be without sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us." - 1 John 1:8 NIV

In this verse, John makes clear the importance of acknowledging our own sin. I think all of us have struggles and sin problems in our lives, and if we claim we do not I would argue we have the sin of pride. It can be easy for us to underemphasize our own sin in our minds. We may not be guilty of claiming to be completely sinless, but we are guilty of lacking acknowledgement of our sins. I would encourage you to take a moment now to stop and recognize a sin in your life that you may have been throwing under the rug - something you may have been ignoring or even justifying. The verse after this one says “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9 NIV). I think that verse 9 follows verse 8 because when one reads the latter and reflects on their life, verse 9 provides a perfect next step and solution to the realization to which they come. Take some time to speak to the Lord and confess the sin that you recognized, asking him to purify you from unrighteousness.

Leading Others to Praise

By Jared Haftel • August 12, 2026
"The heavens declare the glory of God; the skies proclaim the work of his hands." - Psalm 19:1 NIV

I don’t know about you, but every time I see a beautiful sunset, a snow-capped mountain, or a gorgeous blue beach, I cannot help but praise God. One big truth about creation is that it points us to the Creator. Everything that we can see with our eyes in the stunning creation we live in is perfectly designed to lead us to the one who made it all. That is why in Romans, Paul emphasizes that “since the creation of the world God’s invisible qualities… have been clearly seen, being understood from what is made…” (Romans 1:20 NIV). Reading Psalm 19:1 made me realize something about myself. All of creation points people to God, but how good of a job am I doing at the same task? If creation, that doesn’t have a soul or a brain, is able to point people to God, how much more should I be doing the same as one of his beloved children? As I said in the beginning of this post, when I see God’s creation I cannot help but praise him. It is a natural, almost reflexive response to seeing the worship leader that is the world we live in. I will leave you with this question to reflect on as I close this devotional post: When people see you and the life you are living, does it lead them to praise God?
